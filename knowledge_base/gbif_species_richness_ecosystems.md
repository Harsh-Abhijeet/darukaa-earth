# GBIF Species Richness and Ecological Indicators in Agro-Ecosystems

## Section 1: Agricultural Species Richness Baselines
GBIF occurrence records demonstrate that landscape mosaic composition is the primary determinant of terrestrial species richness in agricultural biomes.

Empirical observations across temperate and subtropical zones:
- Monoculture baselines: Intensive single-crop farming zones record average localized species richness of fewer than 15-20 macroscopic taxa per square kilometer (predominantly generalist or pest species).
- Polyculture / Agroforestry baselines: Multi-strata diversified agro-ecosystems record species richness counts exceeding 45-80 distinct taxa per square kilometer, including beneficial parasitoid wasps, solitary bees, and insectivorous birds.
- Habitat edge effect: Agricultural plots located within 150 meters of semi-natural forest or shrubland patches maintain 2.3 times higher native insect diversity than plots isolated >1 km from natural vegetation.

## Section 2: Bioindicator Species and Ecological Health
- Carabid Beetles (Ground Beetles): Sensitive bioindicators of soil compaction and synthetic insecticide use. Density declines precipitously (>70%) under intensive tillage and organophosphate/neonicotinoid exposure.
- Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF): High chemical phosphorus fertilization suppresses AMF spore abundance by 40-60%, reducing plant drought resilience and nutrient foraging capacity.
- Pollinator species richness: Directly proportional to floral phenological diversity. Uninterrupted nectar/pollen availability across at least 8 months of the year is required to prevent colony starvation and local extirpation.
