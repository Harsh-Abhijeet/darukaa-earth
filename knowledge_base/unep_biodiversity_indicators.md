# UNEP/CBD Agricultural Biodiversity & Indicator Framework

## Section 1: Pollinator Abundance and Floral Diversity
Wild and managed pollinators (Apoidea, Syrphidae, Lepidoptera) depend on continuous spatial and temporal availability of flowering resources and undisturbed nesting sites.

Key scientific baselines:
- Monoculture deficit: Large-scale monocultures (>10 hectares) lacking uncultivated field margins create floral resource deserts outside the brief crop bloom window, leading to a 50-70% decline in wild pollinator richness.
- Buffer strip threshold: Installing permanent perennial wildflower buffer strips and flowering hedgerows of at least 3 to 6 meters width along field boundaries increases wild bee richness by 30% to 50% within 2 years.
- Spillover ecosystem service: Pollination services to adjacent entomophilous crops increase by 15-25% in yield and market quality within 200 meters of native vegetation corridors.

## Section 2: Landscape Heterogeneity and Species Richness
Ecosystem resilience is governed by landscape-level beta diversity:
- Threshold of fragmentation: When semi-natural habitat cover drops below 20% of the total agricultural landscape, regional species richness undergoes non-linear collapse.
- Buffer connectivity: Vegetated riparian strips and agro-ecological shelterbelts reduce chemical runoff reaching aquatic habitats by 65-85% while functioning as vital dispersal corridors for predatory ground beetles (Carabidae) and avian insectivores.
- Pesticide impact metric: Applications exceeding 3 pesticide sprays per cropping season reduce non-target soil invertebrates and predatory arthropods by over 45%, triggering secondary pest outbreaks.
